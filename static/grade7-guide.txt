BANSAY | Grade 7 science lesson preparation
Mixtures and solutions • Sample teaching resource

Lesson objective
Help a learner distinguish losing sight of salt from losing the salt itself.

A bridge from English
Ask learners to compare two sentences: "The salt disappeared" and "I can
no longer see the salt grains." Which one reports exactly what they saw?
Everyday wording can describe an observation while leaving its cause open.
The wording exercise leads into the science; it does not prove what happened.

A low-cost comparison
Prepare two clean, labeled, shallow dishes. Add the same amount of plain
water to each. Dissolve a little salt in one and leave the other as a
comparison. Invite predictions. Set the dishes aside to evaporate naturally.
If class time is short, prepare a second pair in advance.

What the observation supports
After the water evaporates, compare the residues. More solid residue in
the salt-water dish is evidence that dissolved material was still present.
Use clean dishes and the same water source so a difference is interpretable.

A question for rehearsal
"If I can't see salt in the water, how could we tell it is still there?"
Let the learner say what they predict and why before showing the result.

Mentor discussion
Identify one phrase a learner could misunderstand. Ask the teacher what
observation they would use to clarify it.
